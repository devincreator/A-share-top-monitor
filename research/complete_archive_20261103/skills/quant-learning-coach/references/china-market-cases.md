# China Market Cases

Use these as default examples. They are for education and research design, not recommendations.

## Broad Index and ETF Cases

- CSI 300 vs CSI 500: large-cap quality/liquidity exposure versus mid-cap growth/cyclicality.
- CSI 500 vs CSI 1000: size exposure, liquidity, drawdown, and transaction cost sensitivity.
- ChiNext and STAR-related ETFs: growth sensitivity, valuation cycles, volatility, and regime changes.
- Broad-index ETF rotation: compare recent momentum, volatility, drawdown, and turnover.

Good puzzles:
- Why might the best recent ETF become the worst next month?
- Should a rotation strategy choose the highest return asset or the best return-to-risk asset?
- What benchmark is fair for a four-ETF rotation strategy?

## Industry and Theme ETF Cases

- Consumption, medicine, finance, semiconductor, new energy, dividend, military themes.
- Use these to teach sector rotation, concentration risk, crowding, valuation sensitivity, and policy/news exposure.

Good puzzles:
- Why can a high-growth theme ETF lose money for a long time?
- Is strong past return a momentum signal or a crowded-trade warning?
- How would you test whether an industry rotation signal survives costs?

## A-share Factor Cases

Common factors:
- Value: book-to-price, earnings yield, dividend yield.
- Quality: ROE, profitability, leverage, earnings stability.
- Momentum: 20/60/120-day return excluding the most recent days when appropriate.
- Low volatility: realized volatility, beta, drawdown.
- Size: market capitalization.
- Liquidity/turnover: turnover rate, amount traded, Amihud-style illiquidity.
- Earnings revision: forecast changes where data is available.

China-specific caveats:
- Suspensions and limit-up/limit-down can make theoretical trades impossible.
- Survivorship bias is severe if delisted names are missing.
- Financial statement release dates must be respected.
- Small-cap and illiquid names can look good before realistic costs.

## Market Microstructure and Rules

Use these rules as learning constraints:
- A-shares generally have T+1 selling constraints.
- Stamp duty applies to stock selling; ETF fee structure differs.
- Limit-up/limit-down and suspensions affect tradability.
- Rebalancing assumptions must define close/open execution and unavailable trades.
- Index constituent changes can create lookahead risk if historical membership is not timestamped.

## Derivatives as Risk Cases

Use stock index futures and ETF options only to explain hedging, basis, leverage, margin, and risk. Do not provide execution guidance.

Good puzzles:
- Why can a hedge reduce beta but still lose money?
- What is basis risk between an ETF portfolio and an index future?
- Why does leverage make drawdown control more important than hit rate?
